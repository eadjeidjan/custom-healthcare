# SickleGuard VOC risk model: report

_Generated by `python -m app.train` on 2026-11-10T19:23:09+00:00 · model `voc-lr-2.0` · training took 38.7 s._

> **All data here are synthetic.** The cohort was produced by `app/simulator.py` from published rates and effect sizes (`docs/research/sim_params.json`, sources and confidence in `docs/research/DATA_SOURCES.md`), many of them low-confidence placeholders. The metrics show how the pipeline behaves when the world works the way the simulator assumes. **They are not clinical accuracy** and must not be quoted as such. The model has to be retrained and validated on real, consented outcome data before any clinical use.

## Task
Predict a **healthcare-utilisation vaso-occlusive crisis** (ED / day hospital / admission, the event stored in the `crises` table; Platt 1991 definition) starting in the **next 7 days**, from data up to today: daily check-ins, IoT devices (wearable, pulse oximeter, smart bottle, thermometer), EMR labs, imaging findings and crisis history. Days inside an ongoing crisis are not scored.

## Simulated cohort vs literature
- 2,000 patients × 365 days from 2025-01-01 (Ghana regions weighted by 2021 census population, genotype mix by climate zone, ~55% children); 682,983 scored patient-days; **1,143 crises**; 7-day outcome rate 1.07%.

Rates for HbSβ0 / HbSβ+ rest on very few simulated patients (rare in Ghana: Kumasi registry Sβ+ 0.3 %); Platt's rates are US cohort means, African utilisation rates may differ.

- Admitted: 59% of crises (research placeholder 60%); median length of stay 4 days (Ghana DHIMS-II median 3 adults, Kumasi paediatric 6); acute chest syndrome in 7.5% of crises.
- Hydroxyurea: 12% of patients (assumed 15% coverage among HbSS, adherence ~60%). Devices owned: wearable 71%, pulse oximeter 54%, smart bottle 40%, thermometer 66%; each device misses 15–30% of days plus outages.
- Pre-crisis drift follows the research file (pain +0.3/+0.8/+1.8 on days −3/−2/−1, resting HR +1/+3, HRV −5/−15%, steps −10/−30%, sleep −0.2/−0.6 h, fluids −0.2/−0.4 L, skin temperature only when infection-triggered). Fever means ≥ 38.5 °C (101.3 °F). Home-managed pain episodes that never reach hospital (1.5 per utilisation crisis) are simulated as realistic false alarms.

**Do devices, labs and imaging help?** In this simulation, with device effect sizes taken from the (weak) literature, the full model reaches AUPRC 0.080 vs 0.082 for check-ins + history alone (AUROC 0.762 vs 0.765): little or no gain. Crisis history and self-reported pain carry most of the signal. Whether wearables add real value can only be answered with real device data, which is the main reason to start collecting it.

Outcome prevalence in the test patients is 1.07%, so an AUPRC of 0.080 is about 7× chance. Calibration slope 1.05 (1 = ideal). Selection rule: best AUPRC among models whose calibration error is within 0.005 of the best-calibrated one.

### Operating points (risk levels)
Sensitivity / specificity / PPV are per patient-day. Alerts = a new alert when a patient crosses the threshold with no alert in the previous 7 days (as the app does: one open alert per patient). Crises warned = a flagged day in the week before onset; some of those patients were flagged all week (persistently high risk, no new signal). Lead time = days from the start of a *new* flagged run inside that week to the crisis.

## Temporal hold-out
Fitted on data before the last two months, tested on 2025-11-01 to 2025-12-24 (all patients; the window includes the start of the harmattan season): AUROC 0.772, AUPRC 0.077, Brier 0.0095, ECE 0.0031, observed/expected 0.78.

| 1,200 | 58,514 | 709 | 0.762 | 0.080 | 0.0087 |

For real data, the research file's sample-size calculation (Riley et al. BMJ 2020, 7-day risk 2%, ~20 parameters) asks for **≥ 6,600 patient-weeks and ≥ 130 crises**, better **≥ 200 crises from ≥ 150–200 different patients**, plus **100–200 crises** at another site or period for external validation. The simulated curve is still rising at ~700 crises, so more data keeps helping; on early real data prefer the simpler logistic regression (fewer parameters, easier to check) and compare with gradient boosting once there are several hundred crises.

## Robustness: how much depends on the assumed pre-crisis drift?
Evidence for a multi-day prodrome visible on consumer devices is weak (one pilot shows a 1-day lead). Re-running with weaker or no drift:

| Prodrome strength | Patients | AUROC | AUPRC | Brier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 1,000 | 0.707 | 0.038 | 0.0098 |
| 0.5 | 1,000 | 0.704 | 0.037 | 0.0098 |
| 1 | 1,000 | 0.704 | 0.040 | 0.0098 |

Strength 1 = the drifts in the research file; 0 = no pre-crisis change at all, so the model can only use triggers, traits and history (same 1,000 simulated patients in every row). The literature-sized drifts change AUPRC by only +0.002: at these effect sizes the short pre-crisis changes are buried in day-to-day noise, and the score mostly ranks patients by baseline risk (history, genotype, hydroxyurea adherence) and current triggers. Real early-warning value depends on prodromes that are larger or longer than published pilots show.

## Inputs by source
- **check-in**: Pain today, Pain rising, Low fluid intake, Short sleep, Stress, Cold exposure, Infection symptoms, Menstruation, Alcohol, Strenuous activity, Missed hydroxyurea, Days since check-in
- **device**: Raised temperature, Low oxygen, Resting heart rate up, Heart-rate variability down, Oxygen below own baseline, Night-time oxygen dips, Skin temperature up, Less active, Less sleep (wearable), Drinking less (smart bottle), Breathing rate up, No wearable data, No oximeter data, No smart bottle data, No thermometer data
- **lab**: Hb below baseline, High white cells, White cells rising, High reticulocytes, High LDH, Raised CRP, No recent labs
- **imaging**: Previous acute chest syndrome, Avascular necrosis, Silent infarct or stroke, Raised TCD velocity
- **history**: Crises in last 12 months, Crisis in last 30 days, Severe genotype, Low foetal haemoglobin, On hydroxyurea, Child, Harmattan season

Missing data never stop a score: a missing value counts as normal and a missing indicator (no wearable / oximeter / bottle / thermometer data in 2 days, no Hb in 90 days, days since the last check-in) tells the model. Device signals are compared with the patient's own 14-day baseline (days −16 to −3).

## Safety rules (always override the model)
Fever ≥ 38.5 °C (101.3 °F) (check-in, or a thermometer reading in the last 24 h); SpO2 < 92% on a check-in or sustained ≥ 15 min on the pulse oximeter in the last 24 h (two or more consecutive low readings spanning ≥ 15 min); chest symptoms; stroke-like symptoms; Hb ≥ 2 g/dL below baseline (lab or check-in in the last 2 days); pain ≥ 8/10.

## Limitations
- Synthetic training data: the numbers show how the system works, not real-world accuracy.
- Must be retrained and validated on real, consented patient outcomes before any clinical use.
- Pre-crisis changes in device signals are weakly evidenced (small pilots); the simulator assumes small 1-3 day drifts, so real device value may be lower.
- Self-reported check-ins can be missing or inaccurate; missing values count as normal (with a missing flag).
- Pulse oximeters can over-read SpO2 on darker skin; wrist skin temperature is not core temperature.
- Red-flag rules always override the model and must be reviewed by the clinical team.
- Not a medical device; supports, never replaces, clinical judgement.
- The model family matches the simulator's structure (multiplicative triggers, small drifts), so simulated performance is optimistic compared with messy real data.
- Thresholds were set for the simulated prevalence; re-derive them from real alert burden and capacity.

## Retraining on real data
1. Collect consented data: check-ins, device readings, labs, imaging and every crisis in the `crises` table (date, hospitalised, length of stay). The label is a crisis starting in the 7 days after the scored day.
2. Build rows with the **same code**: for each patient and day, `features.load(session, patient, as_of=day)` then `features.build(records, day)` (or bulk-load rows and call `features.Records.from_rows`).
3. Fit with `train.fit(algo, X_train, y_train, X_cal, y_cal)` using patient-level splits plus a temporal hold-out, evaluate with `train.evaluate(...)`, then save a bundle like `train.run` does (same keys, a new `version`, e.g. `voc-gbm-3.0-real`).
4. Keep the simulator for pre-training or augmentation only; **report metrics on real data only**, following TRIPOD+AI (calibration slope and intercept, decision curves), and validate externally on another site.
